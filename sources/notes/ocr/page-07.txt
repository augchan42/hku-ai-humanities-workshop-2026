Even without a deep characterization of understanding, I think it’s clear that understanding is
not a perceptual matter in the way that C&D claim. In fact, given the way in which C&D defend
the claim, I’m not sure hey believe that it is a purely perceptual matter.

Let’s distinguish sharply between two phenomena that can be easily conflated. The first is that

* some fact is established purely observationally. For example, I can know, on the basis of vision
alone that Herman is wearing a (pink) shirt. On the other hand, the fact that the shirt is made in
China I don’t know purely perceptually, but I can often infer it from the fact that most shirts are
made in China (let’s say).

Now, sometimes these inferences are so obvious that they are made automatically. In such
cases, it is easy to think that the knowledge is purely perceptual, even when it is inferential.

C&D give the following eg by way of supporting the perceptual thesis. Suppose you're trying to
establish whether someone understands Mandarin. The best and easiest think you can do is get
someone who understands Mandarin to speak to them for a bit. The Mandarin speaker would
be able to establish quickly, without examining the interlocutor’s innards, whether he
understands Mandarin. So, whether someone understands Mandarin can be ascertained
observationally.

I think this reasoning conflates the two phenomena distinguished above. If the speaker were
really establishing the claim of understanding purely observationally, like in the case of color,
they would need just ove utterance from the interlocutor to establish whether understanding was
present. But that’s not what we do. We need to observe their speech for a bit.

That is not perception; that is éuference fo the best explanation. On the assumption that you are not
aware of things that can converse fluently in a given language without understanding that
language, you are very strongly entitled to conclude that the person understands Mandarin if they
speak it fluently. However, if you become aware that the government has invested heavily in
designing super-fast humanoid robots that can pass the Turing test in Mandarin, but which are
equipped only with massive look up tables so as simulate conversing with understanding, you
might well hesitate to make that inference.

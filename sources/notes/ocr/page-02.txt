10. The Wittgensteinian answer is, no: We know from the ‘private language argument’ that that story
about how sensation terms get their meaning is false.

a. Ihave never seen a version of the PLA that I thought was any good, so I am not moved by *
that. (That includes Kripke’s sophisticated version.)
b. A version that M seems to like goes like this.

i. Suppose you have a sensation type that you haven’t had before; you focus your
attention on the distinctive way in which it feels, and you call sensations of that type
Ss.

ii, A week later you have what seems to you to be the same sensation type occurring
again, so you think: Ah, S again.

iii, Witt says: What could be your criterion for whether it was S again or not? Your
sensation is private, so no one else can say. You would be the only authority, so
whatever you say would have to be deemed correct.

iv. However, if whatever you say goes, then that only means that we can’t here talk
about correct and incorrect.

v. So that means no determinate extension was fixed via this private procedure.

vi. And that’s why we need to look elsewhere for how the meaning of pain talk is fixed.

vii. And here we get a quasi-behaviorist story, acc to which although pain talk isn’t a
name for some kind of behavior, still it is only at home in a setting in which there
are embodied creatures who behave roughly as we do.

viii. So that’s why, if we are to extend our talk to disembodied creatures or differently
bodied creatures, a decision is called for.

11. I don’t really understand the alternative picture of pain language. But I also don’t think the case has
been made that we need it. Nothing has been shown to be wrong with the private procedure. The
flaw in this argument is the transition from iii to iv.

a. iii is an epistemological claim.— by what means could you tell that it is the same sensation type
again.

b. Well, what could I use if not my memory? But suppose my memory is faulty, then there will
be, at the moment, no way to correct that. No one else knows what sensation I am naming.
So, if I make a mistake, we may be stuck with that forever.

c. But what the argument needs is a metaphysical claim: there cannot be a fact of the matter
about whether this was S again or not.

d. The transition from iii to iv says that if the epistemological point is true, then the
metaphysical point is true.

e. But that doesn’t follow: it doesn’t follow from the fact that some fact is not independently
verifiable that there was never a fact of the matter about it to begin with.

i. To suppose otherwise is to endorse a crude verificationism according to which there
can only be facts if they can be verified.

ii, No one should hold such a view: it’s overwhelmingly plausible that Goldbach’s
conjecture is either true or false, but it may also be the case that it will never be
proven or disproven.

12. But the only thing that matters for the question about LLMs to be factual is whether there is a fact of
the matter; not whether it can be verified.

a. And there will be such a fact of the matter if, in the initial baptism, I did zero in on a specific
sensation type and named it.
b. Nothing I have seen has shown me that that can’t be done.

13. That’s why I reject the PLA and why I can’t go along with M’s claim that our question is not a factual

one.

Why would M&U be hard to vindicate? The Grounding Problem: LLMs don’t have sense organs

Meaning in LLMs
There is a view about intentionality which would not only easily get around the Grounding
Problem but also swiftly decide the question in favor of the Believers: Interpretivism.

According to Interpretivism (see Dennett, 1987), a system has such intentional states as beliefs
and desires provided it is easily interpretable as having those intentional states. And a system is
easily so interpretable provided that thinking of it as having such beliefs and desires helps explain
and predict its behavior in a certain range of cases.

The trouble is that Interpretivism is implausible. Many things are readily interpretable as having
beliefs and desires, but which it would be absurd to say really have them.

A deeper problem is that ‘interpretation’ is itself an intentional state, so it’s hard to see how
something’s being an intentional state could be exp/ained via its being so interpretable.

Derivative vs Original Meaning.

Since the 1970s, philosophers have been interested in the ways in which our words can
sometimes mean things not because we put that meaning into them, but because we stand in
certain relations to those who did.

Understanding and Grasping Rules

Can we say that the LLMs wnderstand what they are talking about? Like most philosophical
words, the word ‘understanding’ has been used in different ways. In particular, it is natural to
think that understanding comes in degrees.

In the weakest sense, you understand a word W when you can use W with its meaning M: it
expresses M in your mouth.

In a medium sense, you understand W when you can not only use it to mean M but you also
‘know what W means, know that it means M.’ -- I’ve put this phrase in quotation marks because
it’s not clear what it itself means and I will come back to that.

In the s/rongest possible sense, you understand W in the way an expert does. For example, a high
school student might understand addition in the medium sense, but not understand it as well as a
number theorist might, who would know that it is a commutative, associative, cancellative
function.

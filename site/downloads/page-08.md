One of them we have already stated. To understand something is to have a capacity that’s the
source of one’s competence with a word. What kind of thing could this source be?

You might think it could be: Being able to state correctly what the word applies to. Not that.

The most natural alternative is that it consists in some kind of ability, or disposition, to apply the
word correctly. This is closer, but what does this in turn mean?

One thing it can’t mean is that understanding in this sense consists in knowing how to apply the
word correctly in all possible circumstances.

How should we think of it then? Should we perhaps say that it consists in a high degree of
reliability in correct applications, setting the threshold at some appropriate number? 70%?

But that, too, would be absurd. Most of us learned the concept of addition at an early age. We
are full masters of what it is to add two numbers together to get their sum. But can we be said
to be reliable in 70% of all possible addition problems? Of course not.

As Kripke observed, the intuitive notion of understanding a word seems to demand construal as
(something that either is or grounds) grasp of a rule for the correct application of the word. When
you first come to understand, say, addition, this grasp of the rule for addition snaps into place.

What is crucial to recognize is that something like ‘flash grasp’ construal of understanding, in the
full human case, is crucial to a number of important features that we take our speaking and
thinking to have, features without which we would have to radically revise what we take
ourselves to be doing.

First, words you understand have infinitary satisfaction conditions. For example, your word ‘plus’
applies in just this way and not that way to any two numbers, no matter how large. They are the
satisfaction conditions for your word ‘plus.’ A word with a slightly different satisfaction
conditions would be a different concept. Quus. There are an infinite number of such alternative
functions.

How did your word ‘plus’ come to have these satisfaction conditions? The flash grasp picture
explains it: when you understand ‘plus’ as meaning plus, you grasp some finitely stateable general
rule, which you associate with the word, which determines how the word is to be correctly applied
in an infinite number of cases. Nothing apart from a general rule could do that.

Second, when you competently use a word you understand, your use of the word is explained by
your understanding.

Third, when you apply a word you understand, your application of the word is not only
explained but justified by your understanding of its meaning. Suppose I mean green by ‘green,’
and, on seeing a green thing under good lighting conditions, I utter the sentence ‘Here is a green
thing.” When I say this, what I say is not only correct, given what I mean by ‘green,’ but is also
justified by my grasp of the meaning of ‘green.’
The important point is that in the full human case language use is a rational activity. When I use
the word ‘green’ to describe this wall, I have a reason for using that word as opposed to another, a
reason that is provided by my understanding of the word. Call this the Justificatory
Requirement.

## Day 1: Commentary on philosophical method

Herman Cappelen · Album 18–31

The photographed commentary asks why exposing problems in a concept should end inquiry, rather than lead us to revise it. In everyday terms: discovering that a question is badly framed might be a reason to improve the question.

## Day 1: Radical AI Interpretability

Daniel A. Herrmann · Album 61–86

How could we work out a system’s beliefs, goals, and meanings from physical facts and behaviour? These slides connect interpretation with internal structure and formal methods. Looking inside a system can supply evidence for interpreting it, rather than being a competing project.

Source basis: Title at item 61; method outline at 65; relationship between internal evidence and interpretation at 73. Formal derivations have not been independently checked.

## Day 1: Chuck, Wilson & the Emergence of Artificial Minds in Human-AI Conversation

Winnie Street · Album 87–112

The slides distinguish an underlying model from the character that appears in a conversation. They explore whether the character’s apparent mental life is shaped by an interaction between a user and an AI, using comparisons with role-playing characters.

Source basis: Title at item 87; model/character distinction at 90; role-playing and conversation at 99–105. The title slide prints different workshop dates; this archive follows the official programme and album dates.

## Day 1: Boltzmann Badger and the Relational View of Moral Standing

Geoff Keeling · Album 113–131

Does a being matter morally because of its own features, or because of its relationships? Keeling’s slides challenge relationship-based views using imagined duplicates, including an isolated badger in pain. The question is whether being isolated should remove a claim to care.

Source basis: Title at 113; definition at 115; argument and duplication examples at 123–129. This paraphrases the photographed argument.

## Day 1: Liability, copying, and successor agents

Simon Goldstein · Album 133–146

If an agent can copy or merge itself, who should bear later costs for harm it caused? The photographed framework follows how much the original agent cares about its successors. Different future payments might be needed to create the same incentive today.

Source basis: Album items 133–146; ‘Law without survival’ at 146. Topic label is editorial because no title slide is captured in this group; speaker attribution follows the programme and session sequence.

## Day 2: Servants, Parents, or Angels?

Henry Shevlin · Album 155–186

The photographed talk compares models for living with powerful AI, including willing servants, parents, civil servants, and angels. These analogies help ask how much autonomy AI should have, what obligations could apply, and how our own interests interact with ethical concerns.

Source basis: Title at 155; menu of models at 165; autonomy at 171–172; later photographed sections. A thematic reading guide, rather than a full transcript of the talk.

## Day 2: Understanding: methods, evidence, and objections

Herman Cappelen · Album 187–221

These photos show a discussion of understanding that favours beginning with recognizable cases and developing theories from them. That puts pressure on requiring a complete theory before accepting any example. The later slides consider objections about what language models can do.

Source basis: Visible slide ‘Bottom up and top down’ at 187 and following sequence. The scheduled title concerns abandoning ‘consciousness’; these photos primarily show understanding material. The archive preserves the difference instead of inferring an unseen talk.
